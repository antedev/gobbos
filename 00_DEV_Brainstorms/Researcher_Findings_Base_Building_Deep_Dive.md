# Deep-Dive Research: Abstract Base-Building & Societal Dynamics in Games

**Role:** The Researcher  
**Scope:** Cross-media analysis of TTRPGs, video games, and board games implementing abstract settlement/base-building systems, with a dedicated focus on **functional design (zero floor-plan grid bloat)** and **how infrastructure evolution reshapes culture, power dynamics, and society**.

---

## Executive Summary & Core Insights

Base-building systems fail when they degenerate into architectural spreadsheets (e.g., measuring hallway widths and tracking copper-piece maintenance). They succeed when they treat the base as:
1. **A Shared Character Sheet / Engine:** An abstract node-map or card array where upgrades provide distinct functional unlocks (new actions, passive production, risk mitigators).
2. **A Societal Crucible:** A mirror that reflects and accelerates cultural transformation, class stratification, ideological friction, and internal power struggles.
3. **An Adventure Generator:** A system where the base’s needs (and the unintended consequences of its expansion) actively generate the next raid or mission.

## Part 1: How Other Systems Solve Abstract Base-Building

### 1. Tabletop RPGs

| Game | Base System / Form Factor | Key Functional Mechanic | Key Societal / Cultural Mechanic |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Blades in the Dark** *(John Harper)* | **The Claims Map & Crew Sheet** (Abstract node web, Tier 0–4, Vaults, Turf). | Upgrades increase Tier, vault capacity, or cohort quality. Claims are seized via targeted scores. | **Heat & Entanglements:** Higher tier attracts bluecoats and rival syndicates. Faction status determines city underworld politics. |
| **Mutant: Year Zero** *(Free League)* | **The Ark** (4 DEV Stats: Food Supply, Culture, Technology, Warfare). | Players vote on 1 communal **Project** per session (e.g., Palisade, Pig Farm, Scrap Cannon) resolved via progress clocks. | **Societal Evolution:** As DEV stats increase, the Ark transitions from feral mud-eaters to feudal fiefdoms or industrial cults. | **Wicked Ones** *(Ben Nielson)* | **Dungeon Sheet & Lurking Phase** (Abstract room slots, Minions, Traps). | Rooms house minions and brew potions. | **Forbidden Lands** *(Free League)* | **Stronghold Functions** (List of rooms: Forge, Bakery, Library, Watchtower). | Modular functions built on hexes. Each function requires raw building materials, build days, and a **staffed Hireling NPC**. | **Reputation & Attracted Trouble:** Stronghold prosperity draws wandering monsters, greedy bandits, and rival warlords to attack during upkeep. |

| **Ars Magica** *(Atlas Games)* | **The Covenant** (Abstracted lab ratings, Library quality, Vis sources). | Seasons spent researching or expanding libraries directly unlock higher spell levels and enchanted items. | **Covenant Aging & Isolation:** Over decades, covenants warp local peasant economies, attract Church inquisitions, or become paranoid hermitages. |

## Part 2: Video Games

### A. Darkest Dungeon — The Abstract Hub Engine
*   **The Mechanic:** Static screen with clickable buildings upgraded via Heirlooms (Deeds, Crests, Portraits, Busts) from Level 1 to 5. Zero grid or spatial math.
*   **Societal Reflection:** The Hamlet shifts from a dilapidated ruin into an industrial meat-grinder where heroes are disposable fuel.

### B. Cult of the Lamb — Doctrines & Social Decrees
*   **The Mechanic:** Base building paired with Commandment Stone Doctrines (Afterlife, Work & Worship, Possessions, Law & Order, Sustenance).
*   **The Cultural Pivot:** Mutually exclusive choices (e.g., Grass Eater vs Cannibalism, Fight Pit vs Re-education Prison).

### C. Dungeon Keeper — Monster Needs & Faction Friction
*   **The Mechanic:** Imps excavate dirt for functional rooms.
*   **Societal Dynamic:** Minions demand gold on Payday, eat food, and have inter-species hatreds that cause spontaneous tavern brawls.

### D. Frostpunk — The Book of Laws
*   **The Mechanic:** City survival requires passing laws (Child Labor vs Shelters, Soup vs Sawdust, Public Executions vs Faith Keepers). Every structure embodies the community's ideological shift.

## Part 3: Board Games

### A. Frosthaven — The Outpost Campaign Loop
*   **The Flow:** Strict 5-step downtime: Time -> Event -> Operations -> Downtime Actions -> Construction.
*   **Building Cards:** Upgrades flip cards (Level 1 -> 2 -> 3). Outpost attacks damage/wreck buildings, shutting off bonuses until repaired.

### B. Kingdom Death: Monster — Innovation Deck & Milestone Principles
*   **The Mechanism:** Survival/organ resources unlock Innovations (Language, Paint, Ammonia). Population milestones trigger permanent ethical choices (Graves vs Cannibalism, Survival of the Fittest vs Protect the Young).

## Part 4: The Societal Metamorphosis in Gobbos

1. **Emergence of Degenerate Social Castes:** Tinkers in welding goggles vs Swarm Aristocrats controlling beer vs Bone Cultists demanding tribute.
2. **The Greed Dilemma:** Communal survival giving way to personal stashes, Gang Dominance, and tax evasion.
3. **Mob Unionization & Mutiny:** Wealthy veteran Mobs demand higher pay and better gear, striking if the Boss fails their Pitch test.
4. **Warren Decrees:** Communal votes on laws (e.g., The Big Boiling Pot vs Patron Saint Shrine; Free Mob Enterprise vs Iron Whip Tyranny).

---

## Part 5: Golden Takeaways for Gobbos

1. **Visual Node Web:** 6–10 abstract Cavern Nodes (no 5-foot squares).
2. **Strict 5-Step Lair Phase:** Time/Homecoming -> Event -> Labor Allocation -> Boss Downtime -> Construction/Decrees.
3. **Personnel Gating (Elders & Captives):** Advanced functions require retired Bosses or captured experts.
4. **Dynamic Labor Allocation:** Unassigned Gobbo Pool dice become workers (high raid army = zero base progress).
5. **Faction Dominance & Friction:** Gangs claim rooms for exclusive kickbacks, creating inter-gang stakes.
